Whitepaper

Mining, minus the friction

Why Epochia exists

Agent mining projects usually ask users to pay again and again: per share, per boost, per entry. Each payment is a chance to hesitate, misread a price, or give up. Epochia removes the repeat payments entirely. There is exactly one purchase in the whole system: the Epoch Key. Everything after that is a harvest, not a checkout.

The key

The Epoch Key is a non-fungible token on BNB Chain, priced at $0.50 in BNB, capped at 10,000 keys and at most 10 keys per wallet. Minting runs only through a Binance Agentic Wallet agent, and the minting wallet must hold a Binance Agent ID. The key is transferable: whoever holds the key holds its future harvest, and claims already collected stay with whoever collected them.

The harvest

  • One key, one claim per day. Claims accrue per key from the day after the key is minted.
  • Claims stack. Uncollected days accumulate and are paid in one call, at the rates of the days they belong to.
  • No claim fee. Harvesting moves only EPO. The network gas is the only cost.
  • Counting is global. Every claim paid by the contract, to anyone, advances the shared claim counter.

Epochs and halving

Epochia borrows its clock from Bitcoin and swaps blocks for claims. Every 150,000 claims paid network-wide, the per-claim amount halves. There are eleven epochs. When Epoch 11 ends, harvesting ends with it, and any EPO left in the mining reserve is never created into circulation.

Where the money goes

Key sales are the project's only direct revenue. They accumulate in the key contract as BNB, are withdrawable by the project, and seed the initial EPO liquidity pool on PancakeSwap at the token launch. The full allocation is on the tokenomics page.

Honest limits

EPO begins with no market price; the pool sets one at launch and it can move sharply either way. A key guarantees a harvest schedule, not a dollar value. If few keys sell, epochs simply run longer in calendar time, because the halving counts claims, not days.
